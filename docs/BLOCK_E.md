# Block E — presentation renderer and the B0 scripted baseline

**Goal:** a non-learned control that is genuinely hard to beat, reported with the
statistics [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md) demands; and a renderer whose output goes
into wandb and into the thesis rather than into a debugging session.

Consumes Block D (`src/env/core.py`, the observation contract, the extras).
Produces `src/baselines/`, `src/viz/`, `scripts/eval_baseline.py`,
`scripts/render_episode.py`.

Block E is the only block whose deliverable is an *argument* rather than a
capability. B0 exists to answer *"is MARL earning its keep?"*
([`ROADMAP.md`](ROADMAP.md), [`THESIS_PLAN.md`](THESIS_PLAN.md) §3), and
[`MODELS.md`](MODELS.md) turns it into a hard requirement: every architecture
must at least match it. **A weak B0 flatters every result in Chapter 6 and is the
first thing an examiner attacks.** Most of this file is therefore about making B0
strong, and about making the effort spent on it visible rather than asserted.

✅ **Built and measured.** Every number in this file is regenerated by
[`scripts/eval_baseline.py`](../scripts/eval_baseline.py), in the
[`measure_envelope.py`](../scripts/measure_envelope.py) pattern — nothing here
comes from throwaway code, which AGENTS.md forbids.

```bash
uv run python scripts/eval_baseline.py                    # all sections
uv run python scripts/eval_baseline.py --only ladder hops
uv run python scripts/render_episode.py --compare --route 12 --video
```

All figures below: 5 seeds × 64 episodes on the **held-out** route split
(ids 1792–2047), full-difficulty curriculum stage, N = 5, median [IQR] across
seeds. Local runs are CPU/MPS; the numbers are policy behaviour, not throughput,
so they do not depend on the device.

## The bar B0 has to clear

> **Read this first: the rate requirement changed during Block E**, from 5 to
> **15 Mbps**, on evidence this block produced. Every number below is at 15.
> Block D's figures were at 5 and are not comparable —
> [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) has the reasoning and the ledger of what had to
> be re-derived.

Block D's reference points, re-measured at 15 Mbps
(`measure_envelope.py --only policy`):

| policy | mission-capable | observed |
|---|---|---|
| random | 12.2 % | 24.6 % |
| waypoint harness | 26.2 % | 40.4 % |

Three properties of that bar decided B0's design.

1. **The waypoint harness fails at *seeing*, before the radio is involved.**
   `hop_count = 0` means no chain exists, which `best_relay_capacity` returns
   whenever nobody is observing — so a chain exists on only 40.7 % of its steps.
2. **It was set by an *oracle* policy.** `waypoint_policy()` reads `env.hvt_pos`
   off the env object — information no drone senses. A like-for-like B0 beating
   it beats an oracle with strictly less information.
3. ⚠️ **The observation half of the task is one placement decision.**

### Why the harness only sees 40 %, and where the sensor ceiling is

It is not that routes are unsolvable. Same controller, same routes, moving only
the *nearest* drone's station from 15 % of the MCV→HVT line to 0 %:

| nearest drone's station | observed | mean nearest 3-D range |
|---|---|---|
| 15 % of the way back (the harness) | 40.2 % | 197 m |
| **0 % — directly overhead** | **92.6 %** | 99 m |

64 episodes; **every episode was observed at some point under both** (0/64 never
observed; median first sighting 12 s). There are no unsolvable routes.

The cause is geometric and already measured. `linspace(1.0, 0.15, N)` parks the
closest drone 15 % back from the target, which at typical separations is ~180 m
horizontally — and Block D's A2G table gives **38.2 % visibility at 100–200 m
offset at 80 m altitude**. The harness is not failing at anything; it is parked
exactly where the buildings win. (3-D ranges include 78.5 m of altitude
separation, so 99 m is ~60 m horizontal and 197 m is ~181 m.)

Same fact as Block D's solo measurement from the other side: a drone over the HVT
sees it ~100 % of the time. **So `observed ≈ 93 %` is the sensor ceiling and it
is cheap to reach.** What is *not* cheap — and is the whole of the remaining
task — is getting the video home.

## Results

| policy | mission-capable | observed | chain occluded | return |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| random | 10.9 % [1.1] | 23.2 % [1.1] | 15.8 % [1.9] | −163.9 [4.8] |
| waypoint harness (Block D, oracle-fed) | 28.0 % [2.5] | 40.1 % [2.7] | 17.3 % [1.2] | −43.1 [18.2] |
| `B0-geodesic` | 47.1 % [3.1] | 86.0 % [3.1] | 64.4 % [1.6] | 163.8 [19.7] |
| **`B0` — the reported baseline** | **57.2 % [3.5]** | 93.0 % [1.4] | 60.5 % [3.3] | **230.3 [19.5]** |
| `B0-oracle` — stated upper bound | 56.8 % [3.2] | 93.9 % [0.2] | 62.2 % [2.9] | 229.5 [19.8] |

**The two gaps are the finding, and one of them is now zero.**

- **Design effort: +10.1 pp** (`B0-geodesic` → `B0`).
- **Target information: −0.4 pp** (`B0` → `B0-oracle`) — the like-for-like
  variant scores *fractionally higher*, and with IQRs of 3.5 and 3.2 the two are
  indistinguishable. **Perfect knowledge of the target's position is worth
  nothing measurable in this task.**

That vindicates §1's like-for-like decision, and the reason is structural rather
than lucky: **relays do not need the target, they need the observer**, whose
position is in the observation exactly. Only one drone in five needs a target
estimate, and that drone is by definition looking at it.

**`observed` is 93.0 % while `mission-capable` is 57.2 %.** The 36-point gap is
pure relay geometry — the swarm can see the car and cannot get the video home.
That gap *is* the swarm problem this project exists to study, and at the old
5 Mbps bar it did not exist: the two columns were identical for every policy.

### The difficulty is at the end of the episode, where it was designed to be

| t (s) | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 100 | 140 | 180 | 240 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **capable** | 42.9 % | **84.2 %** | 82.9 % | 77.9 % | 67.5 % | 52.6 % | 40.7 % | **35.0 %** |
| observed | 42.9 % | 86.3 % | 93.1 % | 96.9 % | 97.2 % | 98.4 % | 98.4 % | 98.4 % |

Read the rows against each other. `observed` climbs and stays at 98 %; `capable`
**peaks at t ≈ 40 s and then decays by half**. The sensor problem is solved
early; what degrades is the *chain*, as the HVT drives outward and the hop count
rises. That is exactly the escalation [`ENVIRONMENT.md`](ENVIRONMENT.md) designed
the episode around — easy opening, hard ending — and what `γ = 0.997` was chosen
to reach. At 5 Mbps this profile was flat at ~100 % after 60 s.

### Swarm size scales — and hardness is not headroom

| N | `B0-geodesic` | `B0` | **control is worth** | observed | failed *with* the target in sight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 33.3 % | 36.4 % | **+3.2 pp** | 92.0 % | **55.0 %** |
| **5** | 47.1 % | **57.2 %** | **+10.1 pp** | 93.0 % | 35.3 % |
| 8 | 48.4 % | **74.3 %** | **+25.9 pp** | 93.4 % | 19.1 % |

`observed` is flat at ~93 % across all three — the sensor does not care how many
drones there are — while `mission-capable` doubles from N=3 to N=8. **The entire
N-dependence is the relay chain.** At 5 Mbps every N read ~92 % and this was
invisible.

The `geodesic` → `B0` column is the one that decides where to look, and it says
something counterintuitive: **the hardest condition is the least informative
one.** N = 3 has three drones for a three-hop chain, essentially one viable
arrangement, and cleverness buys 3 points. N = 8 has many arrangements and it
buys 26. Read `geodesic` along the row — 33 → 47 → **48** — it barely improves
from N=5 to N=8, because spacing eight drones evenly on a line is no better than
spacing five. `B0` improves because something decides where to put them.

**That gap is the coordination problem, and it is largest at N = 8** — which is
already a zero-shot transfer column, and is the `N_MAX` padding boundary where a
flat MLP should struggle and the size-agnostic rungs should not. Put RQ2's
analytical weight there. Do **not** promote N = 3 to a training condition: it
would confound RQ2's transfer test, cost +15 runs, and — with every drone
load-bearing — remove the slack RQ3 needs ([`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md)).

### RQ3 — the environment forces relay reconfiguration, not observer handoff

Both candidate phenomena, measured against each other per episode
(`eval_baseline.py --only rq3`):

| policy | observer handoffs | gap | lead | **re-roots** | churn | distinct chains | **relay lead** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| random | 7.5 | 39.2 | 1.3 | 36.0 | 67.1 | 13.3 | 1.6 |
| waypoint | 3.4 | 12.6 | 6.3 | 31.8 | 65.6 | 9.7 | 6.3 |
| `B0-geodesic` | 1.3 | 6.8 | 9.4 | 40.3 | 79.2 | 14.6 | **19.7** |
| **`B0`** | **0.9** | 3.2 | 8.1 | **52.2** | 100.6 | 16.6 | **18.4** |

**Observer handoff is rare — ~0.9 per episode for a competent policy.** A drone
parked over the target seldom loses it, so there is little handing over to
explain. **Relay reconfiguration is not rare**: membership changes ~52 times per
episode across ~17 distinct role assignments.

It is also the dynamic driven by **occlusion changing link quality** — RQ1's
subject — where observer handoff is driven by sensor occlusion, the smaller half
of the mission at 15 Mbps. **RQ3 has been re-pointed accordingly**
([`THESIS_PLAN.md`](THESIS_PLAN.md), [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md)), and E3a's
ablation changed with it: it must now zero the `on_path` bit and the edge
features, not `sees_hvt`.

**The metric that makes it a behavioural question rather than a routing one.**
`on_path` changes both because the DP re-selects over stationary drones (the
algorithm) and because drones move into position (the policy). The **relay lead**
column isolates the second: consecutive steps a drone sat *off* the chain while
holding a usable link *to* it, sampled when it was recruited. 0 = picked the
instant it became usable; large = standing by. It orders policies 1.6 → 6.3 →
~19, which is the evidence that it measures behaviour rather than the router.

Two caveats to report with it: a stationary drone accumulates viable time by luck,
so read it **across** policies rather than absolutely; and `B0` scores marginally
*below* `B0-geodesic` (18.4 vs 19.7) because it moves relays far more (churn
100.6 vs 79.2), so its drones spend less idle time sitting viable. Do not
over-read a 1.3-step difference.

Observer handoff is kept as a **secondary** result — the detector works and
discriminates (lead 1.3 random → 8.1 B0), so reporting the rare phenomenon beside
the abundant one is what justifies having chosen between them.

## 1. What B0 knows — the decision that sets what the headline comparison means

THESIS_PLAN §3 describes B0 as "relays placed on the MCV→HVT geodesic", which
needs the HVT's position. There is no neutral choice here:

| B0 is given | Comparison it produces |
|---|---|
| ground-truth HVT state | B0 is handed the hardest part of the task for free. If MARL wins, the win is understated; if MARL loses, the loss is not interpretable. |
| exactly the actor's observation | Like-for-like. "MARL earns its keep" becomes a statement about *control*, holding information fixed. |

**Decision: the reported B0 is the like-for-like one. `B0-oracle` is built too,
as a stated upper bound, and the gap between them is reported as a measurement.**

### The contract, stated so it can be tested

> **B0 is a pure function of `obs["flat"]` and its own carried state. It never
> touches `env.hvt_pos`, `env.mcv_pos`, `env.boxes` or any other env attribute.**

`flat` is the same `(B, N, 108)` tensor the actors consume
([`BLOCK_D.md`](BLOCK_D.md)), so this is enforceable by construction —
`B0.act(flat)` simply has nothing else in scope — and pinned by a test that feeds
B0 an identical `flat` from two differently-seeded envs and asserts identical
actions.

Two consequences worth stating explicitly, because they cut in opposite
directions and an examiner will find both:

- **B0 is allowed to carry memory.** The actors in Block G are feed-forward, so
  B0's per-drone target belief (below) is something the learned policies
  structurally cannot have. That is deliberate: B0 should be the strongest
  non-learned controller the *platform* supports, not the strongest one that fits
  inside an MLP.
- **B0 is allowed roles pre-assigned by index.** [`REWARD.md`](REWARD.md) forbids
  the *reward* depending on agent index and Block G's actors are homogeneous so
  that roles must emerge — but a scripted controller is programmed per airframe,
  and index-based symmetry breaking is exactly what a real one would do. Another
  strength granted to the baseline.

Both are advantages for B0. Say so in the write-up; a baseline that is handicapped
in ways the paper does not mention is the failure mode this section exists to
avoid.

### What B0 may *not* do: share the target's coordinates

The neighbour channel carries a `sees_hvt` **bit**, not the observer's target
fix. That restriction is deliberate — sharing coordinates would dissolve RQ3's
coordination problem — so B0 inherits it. A B0 that gossiped target positions
would have a communication channel the learned policies do not, and the
comparison would be unfair in the other direction.

### Why this costs B0 much less than it looks

**The relays do not need the target. They need the observer.** The chain runs
observer → relays → MCV, and the observing neighbour's relative position is
directly in the observation, exactly and every step. Only the observer itself and
the spare observers need a target estimate — and the observer, by definition of
its role, is looking at the target.

So the like-for-like restriction degrades one drone's information, not five.
Whether that is worth anything is precisely what `B0` vs `B0-oracle` measures,
and that gap is also an upper bound on what any learned policy could gain from
better target inference.

## 2. What B0 does

Batched, on-device, no Python loop over environments — B0 runs at
`num_envs = 256` in the same eval loop a checkpoint will.

### 2.1 Target belief (per drone, carried)

```
if I see the HVT:                 belief ← my own sensor fix          (exact)
elif a neighbour's sees-bit set:  belief ← that neighbour's position  (proxy)
else:                             belief ← belief − my own displacement,
                                           advanced by the last believed velocity
initial belief:                   the cue vector (already in ego, persistent)
```

The neighbour proxy is loose — the observation envelope is ~43 m across-street
but up to several hundred along-street ([`BLOCK_B.md`](BLOCK_B.md)) — and it is
loose in exactly the direction that does not matter, since a relay only needs the
*bearing* to the observer.

### 2.2 Roles, from a local rank

Each drone ranks all `N` drones by distance from its own belief (neighbour
relative positions give the others; ties broken by index) and reads its own rank:

| rank | role |
|---|---|
| 0 | **observer** — fly to directly above the belief, leading it by `v̂·T_lead`. Measured: the overhead station is worth 40.2 % → 92.6 % on its own (the bar section above), so this is the single highest-leverage line in B0 |
| 1 … K | **relay k** — station at `belief + (MCV − belief)·k/(K+1)`, plus a repair offset |
| > K | **spare observer** — an alternative post: one leading the target along `v̂`, one lateral at `±ρ` |

`K`, the number of relays, follows the separation: `K = clip(⌈d/L⌉ − 1, 0, N−2)`
with `d = |belief − MCV|` from the observation and `L` a hop-reach constant. The
escalation is therefore automatic and matches the one the episode is built around
(1 → 2 → 3 hops).

**Hysteresis on the rank.** A drone keeps its role until another beats it by a
margin. Without it the observer role chatters every time two drones swap places
by a metre, which destroys the coverage the role exists to provide — and the
chatter would land directly in RQ3's handoff-rate metric.

**Why spares go to observation posts and not onto the chain.** Because
observation binds and the link does not (the bar table above). A spare parked
where the target is *going* picks it up when the incumbent loses it around a
corner — a scripted, anticipatory handoff, which is also the reference trace RQ3
needs (§5).

### 2.3 Local link repair — the one mechanism aimed at the chain

A relay observes its signed clearance to the MCV (ego) and to each neighbour
(edge features), in metres. It carries a lateral offset and a one-bit search
direction: if the minimum of its adjacent clearances improved since the last
adjustment, keep sliding the same way; otherwise reverse. Bounded to `±A` metres
perpendicular to the chain.

This is a gradient-free hill climb on an observable, smooth, signed signal —
cheap, batched (`torch.where`, no branching), and the only part of B0 that
attacks `chain_occluded` directly. It is also the mechanism most likely to be
what a learned policy discovers, which makes it the fairest thing to give the
baseline.

### 2.4 Altitude and flight

Everyone flies at the **80 m ceiling**, always. Both physical effects point up
and nothing charges for altitude ([`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md)); at the ceiling
the vertical dimension is degenerate by design, so B0 should not pretend to
manage it.

Motion is a **velocity servo**: `v_des = clip(k_p·(waypoint − pos), v_cruise)`,
dash allowed for the observer while closing, then `action = clip((v_des − v) /
(a_max·dt), −1, 1)`. The waypoint harness used a position-P with damping and
never approached the speed envelope; the servo alone should account for a large
part of B0's margin over it.

### 2.5 Launch

Drones spawn on a 5 m ring at 40 m. B0 climbs to 80 m while flying out on a
**fan** — bearings spread by index around the cue. Block D measured that
spreading, not knowing the direction, is what makes acquisition fast (100 % ever
found, t50 8 s, against 59 % for a single bearing), so the fan is the measured
choice and not a flourish. Roles engage as soon as any drone reports a sighting.

## 3. How much design effort B0 gets — and how a reader can tell

Asserting "we tried hard" is worth nothing. B0 ships with an **ablation ladder**,
so the effort is visible as a table of numbers rather than a claim:

| variant | adds | what it isolates |
|---|---|---|
| `waypoint` | — | Block D's harness, oracle-fed, untuned |
| `B0-geodesic` | velocity servo, 80 m, adaptive `K` | THESIS_PLAN §3's literal sketch, built properly |
| **`B0`** | belief filter, ranked roles + hysteresis, spare posts, link repair | **the reported baseline** |
| `B0-oracle` | ground-truth HVT state | the value of target information |

Every rung is a flag on one controller, so the ladder costs almost nothing to
produce and the reported B0 cannot quietly be the weakest rung.

**Tuning budget, stated in the methodology.** B0 has ~8 scalar constants (`L`,
`T_lead`, `ρ`, `max_spares`, `A`, repair step, fan half-angle, `k_p`). They are
coarse-swept and the sweep is reported. Two rules make the sweep honest:

- **Sweep on the training route split (ids 0–1791), report on the held-out
  evaluation split (1792–2047).** `EnvConfig.eval_routes` already exists. A
  baseline tuned on its own test set is not a baseline.
- **The learned policies get an equal or larger budget** ([`MODELS.md`](MODELS.md)
  already requires equal budget across architectures). The comparison must not be
  tuned-MARL against untuned-script.

### The sweep, and what it says about the task

Training routes (ids 0–1791), **5 seeds** × 64 episodes per point:

| constant | values → mission-capable |
|---|---|
| `max_spares` | **0 → 58.7 %** · 1 → 29.0 % · 2 → 28.9 % |
| `repair_amplitude_m` | 0 → 53.7 % · 60 → 58.7 % · **200 → 60.7 %** |
| `repair_along` | **0 → 58.7 %** · 0.15 → 59.8 % · 0.30 → 56.0 % |
| `repair_score` | clearance → 58.6 % · **capacity → 58.7 %** |
| `lead_s` | 0 → 59.5 % · **2 → 58.7 %** |
| `gain_per_s` | 0.3 → 58.8 % · **0.5 → 58.7 %** · 0.8 → 59.5 % |
| `hop_reach_m` | 300 / 420 / **520** / 700 → 58.7 % throughout |

Two constants are load-bearing and the rest are not, which is worth stating
plainly rather than dressing every row up as a decision:

- **`max_spares = 0` — worth 30 points.** Holding drones off the chain as spare
  observation posts is catastrophic. At N = 5 the routing DP uses up to five
  hops, and since `observed` is ~93 % regardless, a redundant observer buys
  nothing while costing a relay. The mechanism was designed in §2.2 on the
  reasoning that *observation binds* — it did at 5 Mbps, and does not at 15.
  The default follows the measurement, against the reasoning that produced it.
- **`repair_amplitude_m = 200` — worth 7 points** end to end (53.7 → 60.7 %).
  Adopted, and with a consequence worth admitting: **at a 200 m lateral search
  B0 no longer places relays *on* the MCV→HVT geodesic.** It places them near it
  and then hunts for a clear line. That is precisely the difference between the
  `b0` rung and the `geodesic` rung, and it is why THESIS_PLAN §3's one-line
  description of B0 no longer describes the reported B0.
- **`repair_along`, `lead_s`, `gain_per_s`, `repair_score` are all within noise**
  (IQRs 1.6–3.2 against differences of ≤1.1 pp). Defaults left where they were.
  `repair_score = "capacity"` is kept on rationale — it optimises the quantity
  that decides the mission rather than a proxy for it, and has the tighter IQR —
  **not** because it measurably beats clearance-scoring. It does not.
- **`hop_reach_m` is inert by construction, not by measurement.** With
  `max_spares = 0` every non-observer relays regardless of separation, so the
  constant is never read.

> ⚠️ **A methodology note, because it nearly caused a wrong decision.** An
> earlier version of the sweep ran at `seeds // 2` = 2 seeds and showed
> `repair_amplitude_m` rising cleanly across its whole range, and
> `repair_score = "capacity"` apparently worth +5 pp. **Both vanished at 4
> seeds.** A sweep too noisy to rank its own options is worse than no sweep,
> because it invites tuning on noise and then reporting it as design. The sweep
> now runs at the full seed count.

## 4. Statistics

`AGENTS.md`: **≥5 seeds, median + IQR, never mean ± std, never a single run.**

One "seed" here is an env seed, which fixes route draws, MCV placement, cue
noise, spawn phase and initial charge. Each seed runs `num_envs = 256` episodes
on the eval split, so 5 seeds is 1280 episodes and costs minutes.

Report **two dispersions**, because they answer different questions and quoting
only one is misleading:

- **across seeds** — median + IQR, the AGENTS.md requirement, and what a learned
  policy's numbers will be compared against;
- **across episodes** — the distribution a single mission draws from, which is
  what the "3.3 % solo" and envelope figures are already quoted in.

B0 is deterministic given the env, so all dispersion is the environment's.

## 5. Metrics — pre-registered, and B0 is the first policy that produces them

From THESIS_PLAN §4. The env already emits most of these in `extras`.

**Mission:** mission-capable fraction (headline) · observed fraction · link-alive
fraction · mean and 5th-percentile e2e capacity · episode return under
`reward()`, so MODELS.md's "must at least match B0" also has a number in the
reward's own units.

**Failure attribution:** `chain_occluded` fraction · hop-count distribution
(§6) · failures split into observation loss vs link loss vs battery.

**Behavioural (RQ3):** observer identity over time → handoff rate, coverage gap
at handoff, **anticipation lead time** (did the successor pre-position before the
incumbent lost line of sight?) · chain re-rooting latency · terminal `Var(B)`.

> The RQ3 metrics have never been computed and their code has to exist eventually.
> **Block E is the right place to write it**, because a scripted policy with a
> *deliberate, known* handoff behaviour (§2.2) is the only test case that can tell
> a working handoff detector from a broken one. A detector first exercised on a
> learned policy cannot be debugged — there is no ground truth to check it
> against. B0 supplies one.

### B0 also validates the reward, with real snapshots instead of stubs

[`REWARD.md`](REWARD.md)'s policy-ranking table (B0 +62.2, formation +21.7,
all-chase −11.0, lazy −44.6) comes from **hand-written `Snapshot` stubs** in
`test_reward.py` — a unit test of `reward.py`, which is the right scope for that
file. But it means the ordering the whole reward design rests on has never been
checked against behaviour the env actually produces.

Add `tests/test_baseline_reward_ordering.py` (cross-module, so it belongs in
`tests/` per the layout convention): score `random`, `B0-geodesic` and `B0`
through the real env and assert the ordering holds. Cheap, and it closes the loop
between the reward's design argument and the environment it will be optimised in.
Label REWARD.md's table as synthetic when the real numbers land beside it.

**Deferred: `τ_c` / `τ_l` retuning.** [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) offers Block
E or Block G. Block E should *report* B0's empirical distributions of
`best_clearance_m` and `e2e_capacity_mbps` — that is the input the retune needs —
but **not change the weights**. The thresholds live in the potential and so can
only affect learning *speed*, which cannot be measured until a learner exists.
Retune once, in G, against the thing they act on.

## 6. Open question 1 — is the escalation under-exercised? **Closed: no.**

[`BLOCK_B.md`](BLOCK_B.md) raised it; Block D measured 2.6 % (random) and 4.2 %
(waypoint) of steps in a 3-hop chain and left it to B0. **The framing was wrong
on two counts, and once both are fixed the worry disappears.**

**Correction 1: chains longer than three hops were never counted.**
`max_hops = R − 1 = 5`, so 4- and 5-hop chains are legal, and `routing.py`'s
divisor is `min(n, reuse_limit = 3)` — **a 4- or 5-hop chain is divided by 3
exactly like a 3-hop one.** The regime F4's rate-division rung acts on is ≥3
hops, not exactly 3.

**Correction 2: the denominator was wrong.** Under the waypoint harness 59 % of
steps have no chain at all, because nobody is observing, and those steps dilute
every share. The right denominator is *steps on which a chain exists*.

Hop distribution under **B0**, eval split, 5 seeds × 64 episodes:

| | 0 hop | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all steps | 6.8 % | 18.7 % | 48.1 % | 13.2 % | 11.2 % | 2.1 % |
| **last third** | **0.3 %** | 4.9 % | 44.9 % | 23.2 % | 21.8 % | 4.9 % |

| conditioned on a chain existing | overall | last third |
|---|---|---|
| chain exists | 93.2 % of steps | **99.7 % of steps** |
| …multi-hop (≥2) | 80.0 % | **95.1 %** |
| …≥3 hops, divisor saturated | 28.4 % | **50.0 %** |

**The escalation is thoroughly exercised.** In the last third of the episode B0
always has a chain, it is multi-hop 95.6 % of the time, and the divisor is
saturated at 3 on 50.0 %. Against the 4.2 % that caused the alarm, that is an
order of magnitude. **No change to the box or the escalation. The question is
closed.**

### And the rate-division rung binds — after the requirement was raised

The pre-registered statistic is what forced the rate requirement up. **At the
original 5 Mbps bar it fired against the rung**: mission-capable was 93.2 % at
`reuse_limit` 1, 3 *and* strict TDMA alike, Δ = **+0.0 pp**, because the chain
bottleneck carried 8× the bar and the divisor flipped 0.10 % of chain-steps. Not
"the rung is small" — the entire divisor was inert.

That is what the threshold change fixed ([`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md)). At
15 Mbps, re-measured:

| duplexing assumption | waypoint | B0 |
|---|---|---|
| no divisor (`reuse_limit = 1`) | 40.1 % | 83.7 % |
| **`reuse_limit = 3` — the model** | **28.0 %** | **57.2 %** |
| strict TDMA (`/n`) | 28.0 % | 56.4 % |
| **Δ from removing rate division** | **+12.2 pp** | **+26.5 pp** |

**The rung now binds decisively.** The divisor flips the outcome on **26.6 %** of
B0's chain-steps, and the chain bottleneck sits at a median 39.0 Mbps — **3× the
bar** rather than 8×, which is the margin band where a `/2` or `/3` actually
crosses the threshold.

**For Block F: F3 → F4 is now expected to be a large effect, not a null.** The
earlier prediction of a null is superseded and should not be carried forward.

### The statistic that answered it

Hop counts are a proxy. What F4 adds over F3 is the *divisor*, so the divisor's
effect was measured directly: B0's own trajectories re-evaluated at
`reuse_limit ∈ {1, 3, max_hops}`, reporting

```
Δ_capable = mission-capable(reuse_limit = 1) − mission-capable(reuse_limit = 3)
```

— the mission-level size of the half-duplex rate division, in the units the
thesis reports. `reuse_limit` is already a parameter of `best_relay_path` and
[`PHYSICS.md`](PHYSICS.md) already asks for the result under more than one
duplexing assumption, so this costs one extra routing call per step and doubles
as that robustness check.

It is a **fixed-geometry** counterfactual: B0 is scripted and does not adapt to
the divisor, so it says whether the rung has anything to act on, not how a policy
would respond to it. That is the right question here — a rung that changes
nothing at fixed geometry cannot change anything at adapted geometry either.

**Pre-registered decision rule**, fixed before the number was seen:

| `Δ_capable` under B0, eval split, ≥5 seeds | verdict |
|---|---|
| **≥ 5 pp** | the rung binds. No change to the box or the escalation. |
| < 5 pp | the rung is close to decorative. Go to the remedy ladder, **before the March 2027 freeze**. |

5 pp was a judgement call, labelled as one. **The rule fired twice, and it is
worth being precise about what happened**: at the original 5 Mbps requirement it
came out at Δ = +0.0 pp and sent the block to the remedy ladder; the remedy
chosen was the rate requirement itself, not the box or the escalation; and the
re-measurement at 15 Mbps gives Δ = +26.5 pp. Pre-registering the rule is what
made that a decision rather than a rationalisation.

## 7. Open question 2 — does the escalation need revisiting? **No.**

§6 closed the escalation question in the scenario's favour: the hop count
escalates exactly as designed, and the chain is multi-hop on 95.1 % of late steps.
**None of the remedies below is needed, and none should be applied.** They are
kept so that a future session which re-opens the question can see what was
considered and what each would have cost.

| # | Change | Would have re-run |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **nothing** ← **the decision** | — |
| 2 | `MCV_MIN_REACH_M` ↑, re-bake the route bank | `frankfurt_box.npz` changes → BLOCK_B's escalation table, D's route/solo/policy sections |
| 3 | `CONGESTION_FACTOR` ↑ (faster HVT ⇒ more separation by t=240 s) | as above, **plus** the drone-speed margin (1.4–1.8× over the HVT) which this erodes |
| 4 | box 1500 → 2000 m | everything: geometry bake, `M` grows so `bench_occlusion` and the throughput gate, W1 and the altitude band, the whole of BLOCK_B |

`dt` and episode length were never on this ladder — both were measured and
rejected in Block D, and a route step is a fixed displacement, so changing `dt`
silently changes the HVT's speed.

**The remedy that *was* applied is not on this ladder at all**, and that is the
point of having written the ladder down. §6's second finding — the inert
rate-division divisor — was never an escalation problem: it was a **link-margin**
problem, and more hops at the same margin still never cross the threshold. Every
remedy above adds separation or hops, so **none of them would have fixed it**.
The lever that did was the rate requirement, 5 → 15 Mbps
([`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md)) — which touches no frozen artefact, and which a
ladder built around "the escalation must be under-exercised" would never have
suggested. Diagnose before prescribing.

Forking would duplicate the geometry drawing, and the whole reason that code is

- **thesis figures**: static, vector, multi-panel, consistent styling;
- **panels that show the claims**: hop count over time, e2e capacity against the

  **which drone is the observer** — the handoff picture RQ3 needs and the first

Matplotlib only. It is already a dependency and `AGENTS.md` requires flagging new

## 10. Correctness — `src/baselines/test_b0.py`, 22 tests

- **The information contract, tested** — the load-bearing one. One scenario's
  observations are replayed through a fresh policy **while a second, differently
  seeded env is live and being stepped underneath**. Any attribute the controller
  reached for (`hvt_pos`, `mcv_pos`, `route_id`) would be the decoy scenario's
  and the actions would diverge. Nothing else stops a tuning session from quietly
  turning the reported baseline into an oracle.
- **The oracle is an explicit channel:** passing `truth` to the like-for-like
  variant raises, and the oracle variant without it raises. It cannot be acquired
  by accident.
- **`reset` clears carried state**, per environment. Belief, roles and repair
  offsets otherwise survive an episode boundary and every new episode starts with
  a belief aimed at the previous route's target — the same class of silent bug as
  Block D's stale potential.
- **Device discipline:** no `.item()`, `.cpu()`, `.numpy()`, no Python loop over
  environments — B0 runs inside the same loop as the env.
- **Batch independence:** perturbing one environment's observation row moves that
  row's action and no other. Catches a reduction across the batch, e.g. a role
  ranking that pooled over environments.
- **Behaviour the design claims:** the observer closes on the target (median
  final range < 200 m against a 78.5 m overhead slant), the swarm pins the 80 m
  ceiling, the relay count escalates with separation, and B0 beats random by
  > 20 pp.
- **The reward ordering, on real policies** — `tests/test_baseline_reward_ordering.py`
  (cross-module, so it lives in `tests/`). REWARD.md's ranking had only ever been
  checked against hand-written `Snapshot` stubs; it now holds for policies that
  actually fly.

> ⚠️ **The spec asked for a strict-monotonicity assertion on the ladder and that
> was wrong.** The measured ladder *is* monotone on the eval split (86.0 → 93.2 →
> 93.8) but `B0` and `B0-oracle` are 0.6 pp apart with IQRs of 0.6 and 0.2 — well
> inside the noise a 5-seed run resolves. Asserting a strict ordering there would
> be a flaky test asserting something the data does not support. What is asserted
> instead: B0 beats random by a wide margin, and the ladder is checked by
> measurement in `eval_baseline.py --only ladder`, not by a unit test.

## 14. Watch out for

- **Quoting a Block D number next to a Block E number.** Block D's are at a
  5 Mbps requirement, Block E's at 15. `mission_capable`, every policy score, the
  solo/W1 table and the escalation figures all moved. The constant is defined
  once in `reward.py` and imported everywhere — including by the three `scripts/`
  that used to keep their own copies.
- **Tuning B0 on the evaluation split.** The sweep in §3 runs on ids 0–1791 and
  the Results table on 1792–2047; keep it that way.
- **A B0 that reads the env.** `env.hvt_pos` is one attribute access away at
  every point in the code, and nothing but the test in §10 stops it.
- **Carried state surviving auto-reset.** Same class of silent bug as the stale
  potential in Block D; it looks like "B0 is oddly bad at episode start".
- **Sweeping at too few seeds.** Cost a wrong tuning decision here: at 2 seeds
  `repair_amplitude_m` showed a clean monotone trend and `repair_score` looked
  worth +5 pp; both vanished at 4. Differences of ≤1 pp against IQRs of 2–3 are
  not results.
- **Taking a median where a mean is meant.** A median across episodes reports a
  clean **0.0 %** for every rare-event metric — `fail_link`, the anticipation
  lead — because most episodes are zero. Seeds get medians; episodes within a
  seed get means. `RolloutMetrics.summary` says so.
- **Reducing a distribution element-wise.** A per-bin median of the hop histogram
  across seeds does not sum to one, which printed "a chain exists on 100.9 % of
  steps". Pool the distribution; take medians of scalar summaries of it.
- **Ranking every drone by distance to the target.** Tried first, and wrong in a
  way only a rollout shows — 55 % against 94 %. §2.2 has the analysis.
- **Assuming the 4.2 % 3-hop figure.** It is diluted by chainless steps and
  excludes 4- and 5-hop chains; §6 replaces it.
- **Believing THESIS_PLAN §3's description of B0.** "Relays placed on the
  MCV→HVT geodesic" describes the `geodesic` rung. The reported B0 searches up to
  200 m either side of that line, and the difference is worth 10 points.
- **B0 beating the learned policies.** Less likely at 15 Mbps than at 5 — there
  are now ~36 points of headroom — but still possible, and it gets reported
  either way. That is what a control is for.
