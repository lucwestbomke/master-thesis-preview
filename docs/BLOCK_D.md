# Block D — batched env core

**Goal:** one env object that steps `num_envs` copies of the mission entirely in
tensors on the GPU, and a measured throughput number that says whether the 45-run
matrix in [`THESIS_PLAN.md`](THESIS_PLAN.md) §3 is affordable.

Consumes Block A (`channel`, `routing`, `energy`, `reward`), Block B
(`data/frankfurt_box.npz`) and Block C (`occlusion`). Produces `src/env/core.py`,
a rewritten `src/env/swarm_env.py` adapter, and `scripts/bench_env.py`.

This block is the gate. Everything downstream — E, F, G — assumes it passed.

Every number below is regenerated by
[`scripts/measure_envelope.py`](../scripts/measure_envelope.py). None of them is
asserted.

## Decisions settled before writing code

Five design questions had no answer in the repo, and each of them changes
results. They were settled on measurement, not preference.

### 1. What the throughput gate counts

**A "step" is one environment advancing one tick, summed across the batch —
env-steps/s.** Not batched calls per second.

The repo said both, and they differ by 1000×. `bench_occlusion.py` and
[`BLOCK_C.md`](BLOCK_C.md) print batched calls/s; THESIS_PLAN §3's budget is
written in transitions:

```
10 M steps per run ÷ 1000 steps/s = 10 000 s ≈ 2.8 h      "~3 h per run"
45 runs × 2.8 h                   ≈ 126 GPU-h            "~120 GPU-hours"
```

That arithmetic only closes on the transition reading, and cost is what the gate
exists to protect. 10 M *batched* steps at `num_envs = 1024` would be 10.2
billion samples per run, which nobody budgeted on purpose.

**The consequence, stated plainly:** under this reading the 1000/s floor is
cleared even in eager mode (1.8 calls/s × 1024 = 1843 env-steps/s), so the floor
stops being informative. **The reported quantity becomes wall-clock for a
10 M-step run, measured end-to-end including the learner, target ≤3 h.** That is
the figure the 120 GPU-hour budget is made of, and unlike the floor it can fail.

**`torch.compile` remains mandatory** under either reading, for two reasons that
survive the unit question:

- **Cost.** 73× on the dominant term is 73× on the 300–500 GPU-hours of
  *development* too, which is the budget that actually binds.
- **Memory.** Unfused, the slab chain holds ~8.8 GB of live intermediates per
  call at `num_envs = 1024` (17.6 GB of traffic). That competes with the learner
  for VRAM on the same card. Fusion removes the allocation, not just the time.

⚠️ All figures above are Apple MPS on a laptop and are a **lower bound**. No
verdict may be declared until `bench_occlusion.py` and `bench_env.py` have run on
the rented CUDA GPU.

### 2. Altitude band — 40 to 120 m

Nothing in the model charges for altitude: `energy.propulsion_power_w` is a
function of speed only, and a full 150 m climb costs 0.55 % of the pack. So the
band is the entire altitude policy — **expect `a_z` to saturate at the ceiling
after climb-out.** Both physical effects point upward:

| altitude | A2A links blocked | HVT visible at 100–200 m offset |
|---|---|---|
| 40 m | 44.6 % | 22.7 % |
| 80 m (nominal) | 31.2 % | 38.2 % |
| **120 m (ceiling)** | **24.6 %** | **48.1 %** |
| 180 m | 10.2 % | 55.9 % |
| 200 m | 2.6 % | 62.7 % |
| 230 m | **0.0 %** | — |

**The ceiling is load-bearing for RQ1.** Above ~180 m the tower cluster stops
blocking anything, F1's A2A component disappears, and the primary result changes
silently. 120 m keeps a quarter of A2A links blocked.

**The floor is a model-validity envelope, not a flight rule.** A quadrotor can
fly at 10 m; this simulator cannot describe it:

| altitude | drone is *inside* a building box |
|---|---|
| 10 m | **37.3 %** |
| 25 m | 18.5 % |
| 30 m | 8.0 % |
| **40 m** | **3.3 %** |
| 80 m | 1.9 % |

`occlusion.segment_clearance` runs with `ignore_endpoint_boxes=True`, dropping
any box that contains an endpoint so a node does not blind itself. That
convention was chosen for a rare case — 1.12 % of HVT route points after the
bridge-deck fix. At 10 m it fires on 37 % of positions, and every one is a drone
**seeing through the building it is standing in**. Separately,
`pathloss_a2g_umi_av_db` clamps `h` to 22.5 m because TR 36.777 UMi-AV starts
there, so below that the channel model silently substitutes a different altitude.

40 m puts containment at 3.3 % with 17.5 m of margin over the model's validity
limit. Document the band as the region where the simulator is trustworthy.

**Also add `W·v_z/η` climb power to `energy.py`** — real physics, cheap, and
traceable. Do not expect it to bind.

**`TODO(verify)`:** the ceiling wants an external citation the way Ptx and
bandwidth have one. The civil UAS operating limit is the natural source and is
believed to be 120 m AGL, but no regulation text has been read for this repo.
Same standing as the TR 36.777 coefficients — **do not cite it until checked.**

### 3. The cue stays, at 24 ego dims, and its justification changes

**Ego observation goes 21 → 24: the existing 21 plus a persistent 3-dim relative
vector to the cue.** Not overloaded onto "relative vector to HVT", which stays
zeroed when unseen exactly as [`ENVIRONMENT.md`](ENVIRONMENT.md) specifies.

The cue currently has **no slot in the 21 dims at all**, so as written it does
nothing — which contradicts its entire stated purpose.

**Its stated justification is also wrong on the facts.**
[`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) rejects blind search because a sparse "found it"
reward over 1500 m² would swamp the learning signal. That argues against a
scenario this project does not have: the HVT starts in a 300–500 m annulus the
drones launch inside. Measured over 512 real routes, five drones flying a radial
fan at cruise speed:

| strategy | ever found | t50 | t90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **no cue**, 5-way fan | **100 %** | **8 s** | 22 s |
| cue σ=150 m, narrow fan | 99.8 % | 10 s | 22 s |
| no cue, all five on one bearing | 59.0 % | 13 s | 46 s |

Uncued is *faster*. What matters is spreading out, not knowing the direction.

**So the cue is not buying exploration. It is buying away the need for
homogeneous agents to learn symmetry-breaking off the neighbour channel** — a
coordination problem no RQ asks about, sitting in Block G, which is the
acknowledged place projects of this shape stall. That is a weaker and more honest
reason, and it is the one that goes in the thesis.

**Persistent, not zeroed on loss of sight.** A stale cue is not misleading:
`grow_outward` builds near-radial routes, so at t=599 the cue is still within
17.8° of the target's current bearing (p90 42.9°, only 0.4 % of routes past 90°),
and flying to it beats sitting at the MCV in 99 % of routes. **It decays in
range, not in direction.**

**How the policy knows it is stale**, without a clock: mostly it does not need
to, per the above; the discriminator it actually needs — cue or neighbour
channel — is directly observable from own and neighbours' `sees_hvt` flags; and
for the residual, distance-to-MCV is already in the 21 dims and is strongly
monotone in episode phase (404 → 709 → 1011 → 1333 m).

**No time feature, in the actor or the critic.** Pardo et al. (2018) separate
*time-limited* tasks, where the horizon is part of the problem and remaining time
belongs in the observation, from *time-unlimited* tasks, where the limit only
diversifies training and the correct treatment is partial-episode bootstrapping.
This mission is the second kind — 240 s covers the hop escalation, nothing about
the mission ends there — and Block A already took that stance:
`reward.shaping`'s docstring says *"Truncation is not terminal — bootstrap the
value there instead."* Adding a time feature would let the policy condition on an
artificial horizon and contradict a decision already made correctly. What
replaces it is a **requirement**: the skrl wrapper must keep `terminated` and
`truncated` distinct and bootstrap at truncation. Wrappers routinely collapse the
two, so this is asserted in the smoke test at D2.5.

**The no-cue condition becomes a one-flag ablation**, not
[`ENVIRONMENT.md`](ENVIRONMENT.md)'s "optional stage 5". It is the direct answer
to *"isn't the cue a cheat?"*, and it is now cheap and credible.

### 4. Sensor — 360°, no field of view

`sees = (clearance ≥ 0) & (range ≤ 830 m)`. No pointing state, no attitude, no
slew. Documented in the methodology as a **gimballed-turret assumption**: such a
turret does have continuous 360° azimuth and full downward elevation, and holds
lock once tracking. Modelling it properly needs a pointing state plus either two
more action dims or an auto-tracker, which is scope creep into gimbal control —
excluded for the same reason as rigid-body flight dynamics.

**The caveat is load-bearing for exactly one thing.** 360° is optimistic for
*search*, not for tracking, so it props up the no-cue ablation above and nothing
else. The write-up must say "unaided acquisition is feasible given a gimballed
sensor with negligible slew cost", not "the cue is unnecessary".

**Rejected: a minimum depression angle.** It would make that ablation airtight
rather than caveated, and it is cheap to compute — but it fixes one unsourced
constant (830 m) by adding a second, and a *binding* sensor parameter is exactly
what [`BLOCK_B.md`](BLOCK_B.md) identifies as confounding RQ1 with sensor
specification. If the no-cue result ever needs hardening, do it as a
**sensitivity analysis** over two or three angles — stronger evidence than any
asserted value, and the same move `routing.py` already makes with `reuse_limit`.

**Rejected: a fixed downward camera cone.** Models the wrong hardware and
duplicates the roofline-clearance constraint.

### 5. `tx_mask` under the scheduled MAC

[`PHYSICS.md`](PHYSICS.md) requires the mask to hold only the transmitters active
in the evaluated slot — one node for a ≤3-hop reuse-3 chain. But the capacity
matrix must be built *before* routing picks the path, so no single `(B, R)` mask
can be "only i" for every link at once.

**Evaluate every candidate link as if alone in its slot:** intra-swarm
interference is identically zero and `SINR = S / (J + N₀)`. Pin it with a test
asserting equality against `sinr_db` called with a one-hot `tx_mask` per
transmitter. Keep an `interference_mode: {"scheduled", "concurrent"}` seam for
the duplexing robustness check PHYSICS.md asks for; wire only `"scheduled"` in D.

## Why the mission still needs a relay chain

Worth recording, because "the drones fly above the rooftops, so why relay?" is
the first question anyone asks of this scenario, and the answer decides whether
RQ1 is a real question.

**Altitude buys A2A, not A2G.** Both endpoints that matter are on the ground: the
MCV is parked in a street, the HVT drives in one. The ground-LoS radius is
`(W/2)·h/H_b` — 50 m at 80 m altitude, 75 m at 120 m. A clear ray to a ground
node 1400 m away would need an altitude of **2240 m**: above the band, above any
regulation, and outside TR 36.777's validity. Climbing makes the *middle* of the
chain cheap and leaves both *ends* exactly as hard.

Drone at 120 m, 1400 m from the MCV:

| | capacity |
|---|---|
| single hop, **clear** ray | **46.1 Mbps** |
| single hop, **blocked** ray | 4.8 Mbps |
| single hop, blocked + jammer at the MCV | 4.8 Mbps |
| 2-hop: A2A 1200 m + A2G 200 m | **27.6 Mbps** |
| threshold | 5.0 Mbps |

**The chain is required by blockage, not by range.** With a clear ray one drone
closes 1400 m at 9× the threshold; it fails only because that ray grazes at 4.8°
and is essentially never clear. **This is what makes RQ1 a real question** — if
the chain were needed for range, an F0 connectivity-radius model would capture
the requirement perfectly and there would be no gap to measure. Because it is
needed for blockage, a radius-trained policy routes the long direct link straight
through a building and fails, which is precisely the hypothesis.

It also reinforces the 120 m ceiling: 80 → 120 m widens the ground-LoS radius
from 50 to 75 m. Push it higher and the constraint the mission rests on starts
dissolving.

**Two things to reconcile before the freeze**, both listed under "Measure this
while you are here":

- `scenario_design.py` uses an analytic canyon rule (LoS within 0.625×altitude)
  that is **more conservative than the real map** — measured visibility at
  100–200 m offset is 48 % at 120 m where the rule says 0 %. "The solo drone
  fails" therefore needs re-confirming on actual geometry.
- `scenario_design.py` jams the *drone* (overhead the HVT, LoS to the jammer);
  the table above jams the *MCV* (the receiver of the video feed). Both are
  defensible for different link directions; the env computes jammer power at
  every node properly and makes the question moot, but the two figures should
  not be quoted side by side without saying which is which.

## Occlusion is ~all of the cost

At `num_envs = 1024`, `N = 5`:

| Stage | Work per step | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **occlusion** | 1024 × 21 links × 5120 boxes = **110 M** segment–box tests | ~99 % |
| channel | ~15 `(1024, 6, 6)` elementwise/log ops | <1 % |
| routing DP | 5 × `(1024, 6, 6)` reductions | <1 % |
| reward, energy | ~20 `(1024, 5)` ops | <1 % |
| observations | gathers into `(1024, 5, 24)` + `(1024, 5, 4, 9)` | <1 % |

So `bench_occlusion.py`'s number is very nearly the answer, and the env's job is
to not *add* a second bottleneck — host syncs, Python loops over envs, graph
breaks. Cost scales as `K(K−1)/2` with `K = N + 2`: **N=3 → 10 links, N=5 → 21,
N=8 → 45**, so RQ2's transfer evaluation at N=8 costs 2.1× a training step.

## Node layout

Two node sets, deliberately different sizes. Confusing them feeds the HVT into
the routing DP as a relay.

```
geometric nodes  K = N + 2    0 … N-1 drones,  N = MCV,  N+1 = HVT
radio nodes      R = N + 1    0 … N-1 drones,  N = MCV
```

Occlusion runs on all `K` — the drone↔HVT rays serve **both** the sensor and the
jammer path, so they are computed once. The HVT is never a radio node: it is the
target and the jammer, not a relay. `routing.best_relay_capacity(dst_index=N)`.

**Link class** from indices, as a static `(R, R)` bool: both `< N` → A2A
(`pathloss_a2a_db`), otherwise A2G (`pathloss_a2g_umi_av_db`). Exactly one
endpoint of an A2G link is aerial, so `h_uav = max(z_i, z_j)` is correct and
branch-free.

## The step pipeline

1. **Kinematics.** `a = action.clamp(-1,1) * max_accel_ms2`; `v += a·dt`, clipped
   to `DRONE_DASH_MS`; `p += v·dt`. Clamp x,y to ±750 m and z to **[40, 120] m**.
   Clamping must zero the offending velocity component, or drones press into the
   wall and the energy term charges for motion that did not happen.
2. **HVT.** `t += 1`; `hvt_xy = route_xy[route_id, idx]` with
   `idx = (t * speed_scale).long().clamp(max=599)`; `z = 1.5`. `hvt_vel` by
   finite difference of the same lookup.
3. **Occlusion.** `pairwise_clearance(pos_K, boxes, heights)` → `(B, K, K)`. One
   call; everything else reads from it.
4. **Sensor.** `sees[b,i] = (clearance[b,i,HVT] >= 0) & (range <= 830 m)`. The
   range test essentially never binds (0.2 % of sightlines) and exists so the
   non-binding claim in [`BLOCK_B.md`](BLOCK_B.md) is literally true.
5. **Channel.** distances → per-class path loss → `received_power_dbm` → jammer
   power at each radio node (A2G from the HVT, gated by the same clearance,
   ×`jammer_on`) → `sinr_db` → `capacity_mbps`. `(B, R, R)`.
6. **Routing.** `best_relay_capacity(cap, source_mask=sees, dst_index=N,
   max_hops=R-1)` → `(B,)`, **plus path membership** (below).
7. **Reward.** Assemble `Snapshot`, call `reward(...)` with the cached previous
   potential. `next_is_terminal` = battery death only.
8. **Done.** `terminated = (battery <= 0).any(dim=-1)`; `truncated = t >=
   episode_len` (per-env). Mission failure goes in `extras`, never in either flag.
9. **Auto-reset** done envs in place with `torch.where` on full-batch tensors —
   never boolean indexing, which produces data-dependent shapes and breaks the
   compiled graph.
10. **Observations.**

### Routing must also return the path

`best_relay_capacity` returns only `(B,)` capacity today. Block D needs node
membership for two things already promised:

- the ego feature **"on active relay path"** ([`ENVIRONMENT.md`](ENVIRONMENT.md));
- the failure-attribution metric **"fraction of steps where the intended chain
  passes through an occluded link"** ([`THESIS_PLAN.md`](THESIS_PLAN.md) §4),
  which is the direct signature of a radius-trained policy and therefore RQ1's
  most load-bearing diagnostic.

Extend the DP with back-pointers: store `argmax_i` per hop, record which hop
count won, walk back with ≤`max_hops` batched gathers. No Python loop over
environments. Real work, and it belongs here rather than in G.

## Remaining design decisions

**Curriculum axes as per-env tensors.** Stages are sampled per environment (the
~20 % mixed-earlier-stages rule), so every axis is `(B,)`: `episode_len`,
`speed_scale`, `jammer_on`, `battery_scale`, `cue_sigma`. All shape-stable.

⚠️ **[`ENVIRONMENT.md`](ENVIRONMENT.md)'s stage table is quoted in free-flow
class speeds (8.3 / 13.9 m/s) but the baked route bank already has
`CONGESTION_FACTOR = 0.70` applied** — realised speeds are median 5.8 m/s, max
9.7 m/s. Stage 2's "residential 8 m/s" is already above the bank's median.
Restate the stages as a `speed_scale` on the baked route (0.0 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0)
and amend that table, or the curriculum silently has no speed axis. Battery
multipliers become a `capacity_scale` divisor on drain, not a charge above 1.0 —
the observation must stay in `[0, 1]`.

**Spawn geometry.** Drones launch parked on the MCV. Exactly co-located gives
zero-length links (`fspl_db` clamps at 1 m) and puts every drone in the same
building box. Use a deterministic 5 m ring at z = 2 m with a per-episode random
phase, then let them climb into the band.

**Held-out routes.** The bank holds 2048 routes and a 10 M-step run is ~16 700
episodes, so each is reused ~8×. Split it: **ids 0–1791 train, 1792–2047
evaluation-only.** Routes were sampled i.i.d., so the split is valid, and it
costs nothing now. If transfer ever looks like map memorisation, the fix already
scoped in [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) is to sample MCV positions from all 858
densified road points instead of the 121 junctions.

**Auto-reset semantics.** Return the observation of the **new** state, with the
pre-reset final observation in `extras["final_observation"]` for bootstrapping.

⚠️ **The cached previous potential must be recomputed after every reset.**
`reward.shaping` reads `Φ(s)` from the previous step; carrying a pre-reset `Φ`
across an episode boundary injects a large spurious shaping term on the first
step of every new episode. It is silent, it survives every single-step unit test,
and it biases exactly the property PBRS was chosen to guarantee.

## What to produce

```python
class BatchedSwarmEnv:
    def __init__(self, num_envs, num_drones=5, artefact=..., device="cuda:0",
                 compile_step=True, seed=0): ...
    def reset(self) -> dict[str, Tensor]: ...
    def step(self, actions: Tensor) -> tuple[dict, Tensor, Tensor, Tensor, dict]:
        # actions   (B, N, 3) in [-1, 1]
        # obs       dict of tensors, below
        # reward    (B, N)
        # terminated(B,)  battery death only
        # truncated (B,)  episode_len reached
        # extras    mission_capable, e2e_capacity_mbps, hop_count,
        #           chain_occluded, sees_any, altitude, final_observation, ...
```

Observation contract — E, F and G all build against this, so it is fixed here:

| key | shape | who reads it |
|---|---|---|
| `ego` | `(B, N, 24)` | actor |
| `neighbour` | `(B, N, N-1, 9)` | actor |
| `edge` | `(B, N, N-1, 2)` | GNN rung only — capacity, clearance |
| `state` | `(B, S)` | critic, training only |
| `flat` | `(B, N, 108)` | skrl rollout storage — see below |

**`flat` is packed at `N_max = 8`**: 24 ego + 7×9 neighbour + 7×2 edge + 7
validity bits = 108. skrl's rollout storage wants a fixed-shape tensor per agent,
and [`MODELS.md`](MODELS.md) requires max-N padding so the MLP rung can be
evaluated at `N ∈ {3,8}` at all. Models unpack it; the structured keys stay
available for debugging and for PyG batching.

Neighbour ordering is a precomputed `(N, N-1)` index table, fixed for the run.

Everything stays on `device`. No `.item()`, `.cpu()`, `.numpy()`, and no Python
loop over environments anywhere in `step()`.

## Throughput: measure it in this order

[`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md) says measure before building on top. The concrete
reading:

| | Built | Benchmarked | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D−1** | **nothing — this comes first** | `scripts/bench_occlusion.py` + the full test suite, on the rented CUDA GPU | occlusion is ~99 % of the step, so this is ~99 % of the gate answer. It also runs the CUDA code path for the first time ever |
| **D0** | kinematics + occlusion + channel + routing on random state. No obs, no reward, no reset. | first `bench_env.py` run | is the floor plan viable, and at which `num_envs` |
| **D1** | + reward, observations, auto-reset, curriculum | re-run; expect **≤2×** D0 | did the scaffolding add a second bottleneck |
| **D2** | + PettingZoo adapter | API compliance only | — |
| **D2.5** | + custom skrl multi-agent wrapper, **smoke only** | end-to-end env+learner throughput | does skrl accept this observation contract, and does it bootstrap at truncation |
| **D3** | — | **re-run everything on the rented CUDA GPU** | **the verdict** |

**D−1 comes before any env code is written**, and it is about an hour of rented
GPU time. Occlusion is ~99 % of the step cost, so `bench_occlusion.py` — which
already exists — is very nearly the whole gate answer, and every design choice
below the gate is downstream of that number. Writing ~800 lines of env first and
discovering the kernel does not fuse on CUDA would waste the block.

It is also the **first time this codebase has ever run on CUDA**:
[`BLOCK_C.md`](BLOCK_C.md) records the CUDA path as untested. So run the full
suite there too, not just the benchmark. What to do, in order:

1. Rent the GPU you actually intend to train on — the number does not transfer
   between cards. Note eager occlusion needs ~8.8 GB of live intermediates at
   `num_envs = 1024`, so a 24 GB card is fine compiled but tight for the eager
   comparison at 2048.
2. `git clone` and `uv sync`. `data/frankfurt_box.npz` is committed, so there is
   nothing else to fetch and no network calls at runtime. Check that `uv sync`
   resolved a CUDA torch build rather than the CPU wheel.
3. `uv run pytest` — 158 tests, on CUDA. Device bugs surface here, cheaply.
4. `uv run python scripts/bench_occlusion.py`, then sweep `--chunk 256 512 1024
   2048` separately: the chunk size was tuned against MPS's memory hierarchy and
   there is no reason the same value is optimal on CUDA.
5. Record the table in [`BLOCK_C.md`](BLOCK_C.md), **in both units**, replacing
   the "must be re-run on CUDA" caveat with the real numbers.

Three outcomes, three different plans:

| compiled throughput | what it means | what to do |
|---|---|---|
| comfortably above the gate | expected — MPS was a lower bound | proceed to D0 as specified |
| marginal | the env budget is thin before the learner is even added | tune `chunk`, then reconsider `num_envs`; D0 decides the operating point |
| `torch.compile` fails or does not fuse | **maybe** Block C reopens — check the toolchain first | see below |

⚠️ **Do not trigger the Block C reopen on a compile failure alone.** Inductor
emits Triton kernels, and Triton support for very recent GPU architectures lags
the hardware. A compile failure on a brand-new card is more likely a toolchain
problem than a flaw in the kernel design. **Re-test on an older, well-trodden GPU
(A100 / L40S / A6000) before concluding anything** — that is one more rented hour
against a decision that would otherwise cost weeks of broad-phase work.

Record the full provenance next to the numbers: GPU model, driver, CUDA version,
PyTorch version, Triton version. Throughput figures without them are not
reproducible, and they are going in the thesis.

If D1 is much worse than 2× D0, the cause is a graph break or a host sync, not
arithmetic — find it before adding anything else.

**D2.5 is not training.** No curriculum, no learning claim, nothing reported. It
exists to answer two questions while the observation contract is still free to
change: does skrl's rollout storage accept `flat`, and does the wrapper keep
`terminated` and `truncated` distinct. Discovering either in Block G means
rewriting a contract that E and F have already been built against, in the month
most likely to be lost to curriculum tuning.

`scripts/bench_env.py` must report:

- throughput in **both** units, labelled, so decision 1 cannot be re-litigated;
- **wall-clock for a 10 M-step run** — the number the budget is made of;
- a per-stage breakdown, confirming occlusion is still ~99 % and nothing new has
  appeared beside it;
- peak allocated VRAM at each `num_envs` — the learner shares the card;
- a sweep over `num_envs ∈ {256, 1024, 4096}` and `N ∈ {5, 8}`;
- eager vs compiled, compile warm-up excluded from timing and reported separately;
- **`PROVISIONAL — not CUDA` in place of any verdict on CPU/MPS.**

## `torch.compile`

- Compile one pure function `_physics(state, actions) -> (state, metrics)` with
  `dynamic=False` and **`fullgraph=True`**. `fullgraph` is the point: a graph
  break is exactly the silent degradation the device rules forbid, so it should
  raise rather than quietly fall back.
- Keep `reset()` and observation assembly outside at first; fold them in at D1
  only if the measurement says they matter.
- Recompilation triggers on any change of `num_envs` or `N`. Evaluation at
  `N ∈ {3, 8}` is a separate compile — expected, but budget the warm-up.
- If launch overhead dominates at small `num_envs` on CUDA, the next lever is
  CUDA graphs; after that, Block C's unbuilt spatial broad phase (~100× fewer
  candidate boxes). Neither is needed unless measured.

## Correctness

Co-located `src/env/test_core.py` plus cross-module tests in `tests/`.

**Physics wiring** — that the env calls Block A the way the docs say:
- HVT position matches `route_xy[route_id]` exactly at `speed_scale = 1`.
- Battery drain over 600 steps of hovering lands near the 7 % `PHYSICS.md`
  predicts; bound it at 25 %, as `test_energy` does.
- Scheduled-MAC SINR equals `sinr_db` with a one-hot `tx_mask`, per decision 5.
- A2A/A2G class assignment matches the index rule for every pair.
- Extracted relay path is consistent with the capacity the DP returned.
- Positions never leave `[40, 120] m` in z or ±750 m in x,y; clamping zeroes the
  velocity component.

**Env semantics:**
- Episodes are exactly `episode_len` steps; mission failure never terminates.
- Battery death terminates, and `Φ = 0` is applied there.
- Auto-reset leaves no state from the previous episode — **including the cached
  potential**.
- Same seed → identical trajectories (run-level determinism).
- **Batch independence:** stepping `B=4` with four identical seeds gives four
  identical trajectories, each matching `B=1`. This is what catches an accidental
  reduction across the batch dimension, which is otherwise invisible.

**Device discipline:**
- Run the step under `torch.cuda.set_sync_debug_mode("error")` in a CUDA-only
  test. Any hidden `.item()` or host copy then raises instead of quietly costing
  a sync — it makes the hard rule enforceable rather than aspirational.
- ⚠️ **Parameterise the numerical tests over device.** Discovered at D−1: *no
  test in this repo mentions `cuda` or sets a device* — all 158 run on the
  default device, so passing them on a CUDA box proves the code runs on Linux and
  nothing about the CUDA path. [`BLOCK_C.md`](BLOCK_C.md)'s "works on CPU and
  MPS" rests on someone having run them there by hand. Add a `device` fixture
  that skips unavailable backends, so `uv run pytest` on the rented GPU actually
  means something. Until that exists, the only CUDA correctness evidence is
  `bench_occlusion.py`'s parity check (CUDA vs CPU, compiled vs eager), which was
  added for exactly this reason and gates its own throughput numbers.

**Adapter:** `pettingzoo.test.parallel_api_test`, plus a test that the adapter at

## Measure this while you are here

Extend [`scripts/view_episode.py`](../scripts/view_episode.py) to draw the
drones, the chosen relay chain and per-link clearance, then run a random and a
scripted policy through it. Five questions, all of which decide something:

1. **Does the solo drone actually fail?** `scenario_design.py`'s analytic canyon
   rule is more conservative than the real map (0 % vs a measured 48 % visibility
   at 100–200 m offset from 120 m). Run a 1-drone policy and a chain over the
   route bank and compare e2e capacity distributions. If the solo drone succeeds
   materially more than a few percent of the time, the box or the escalation
   needs revisiting **before the March freeze**.
2. **Is the 3-hop regime exercised?** [`BLOCK_B.md`](BLOCK_B.md) flagged this for
   Block D: escalation lands at median 1333 m against a 1400 m target, and 45 %
   of route steps are already beyond 1000 m. Record `hop_count` over a full bank
   sweep. If 3-hop chains are rare, the premise is under-exercised.
3. **Altitude.** Does the policy pin to the 120 m ceiling? If so that is a
   finding about the reward, to be reported rather than tuned away.
4. **Mission-capable fraction** for a random and a scripted policy — the headline
   metric needs a floor and a ceiling before Block G can be judged.
5. **The 333-step lingering route.** [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) leaves it
   open: `grow_outward` may stall where the graph is sparse. The bridge decks are
   gone and the worst route is now 29 steps, but the *timing* was never
   re-checked, and a running env plus the viewer is the cheapest place to close
   it.

## Watch out for

- **A host sync hidden in an `if` on a tensor.** `if terminated.any():` is a
  `.item()` in disguise and will not look like one in a diff. Use `torch.where`.
- **Boolean-mask auto-reset** produces data-dependent shapes and silently breaks
  `fullgraph=True` — or worse, compiles into a recompilation loop.
- **The stale potential across resets.** Silent, and it attacks exactly the
  property PBRS was chosen for.
- **A wrapper that collapses `terminated` into `truncated`.** With no time
  feature in the observation, correct bootstrapping at truncation is what keeps
  the value function unbiased. Assert it.
- **The MCV at z = 2 m in `pathloss_a2g_umi_av_db`.** The function clamps `h` up
  to 22.5 m, outside TR 36.777's validity for a ground-to-ground link. It matters
  for the jammer→MCV path and errs toward *more* jam power at the MCV, i.e.
  pessimistic. Acceptable, but state it in the methodology.
- **`num_envs` as a free parameter.** It changes the effective batch the learner
  sees per rollout, so it is not purely a throughput knob — fix it before the
  curriculum tuning in March, not during.
